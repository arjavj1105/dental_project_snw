Myths & FAQs

Let's bust some common dental myths and answer your frequently asked questions

❌ Common Dental Myths

Tooth extraction is harmful to eyes +

✓ The Truth:

This is completely FALSE! There is no nerve connection between teeth and eyes. Tooth extractions are safe procedures performed under proper anesthesia.

All baby teeth cavities need extraction +

✓ The Truth:

Not necessarily. Baby teeth can be treated with fillings or other restorative methods to preserve them until they naturally fall out.

Brushing harder cleans better +

✓ The Truth:

False! Hard brushing can damage your gums and enamel. Gentle, proper technique is more effective than aggressive brushing.

White teeth are always healthy teeth +

✓ The Truth:

Not true. Teeth color doesn't determine health. You can have white teeth with cavities or healthy teeth with natural coloring.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How many times should I brush my teeth? +

You should brush your teeth at least twice a day - morning and night. Ideally, brush 30 minutes after meals to prevent enamel damage.

Which toothpaste is better? +

Look for toothpaste with fluoride and the ADA seal. Choose based on your needs: sensitivity, whitening, or cavity prevention. Consult your dentist for personalized recommendations.

When should children start brushing? +

Children should start brushing when their first tooth erupts (around 6 months old). Use a soft toothbrush and fluoride-free toothpaste for babies.

When should children start eating sugar? +

Avoid giving children sugary foods before age 2. After that, limit sugary foods and drinks, especially between meals. Rinse mouth with water after consuming sweets.

How long should bottle feeding continue? +

Bottle feeding should ideally stop by age 12-18 months. Prolonged bottle use, especially at night, can cause tooth decay and developmental issues.

When do baby teeth erupt and fall out? +

First teeth typically erupt around 6 months. They start falling out around age 6. Baby teeth are usually all gone by age 12, replaced by permanent teeth.

Why do some teeth remain impacted? +

Teeth can be impacted due to insufficient jaw space, bone density, or abnormal tooth positioning. Wisdom teeth are commonly impacted and may need extraction.

What to do if a tooth gets knocked out during sports? +

Quickly find the tooth, hold it by the crown (not the root), rinse gently with milk or saline solution, and try to reinsert it. If not possible, place it in milk and visit the dentist immediately!
